The short version

What we covered

The call opened with the team reviewing Allan’s recent call with a content creator who has been making video game content for about six years and mostly does it for fun. Allan said he had no real idea what to do with the lead, and the call ended with the prospect saying he needed to think about it. Allan played the recording from the two minute mark so everyone could hear how it opened. Harrison said he would not turn it into a content call and would instead push back harder on why the prospect wants to do content at all, and what he expects to get from it that his current situation does not give him. JJ agreed that works and said the goal is to call people out on what they are really saying, and offered to send the team one of his own past sales calls to watch. Once Allan’s review wrapped, JJ moved to a debrief with Iwin and Chris on a call Iwin took the day before, where the prospect pushed back on his motivation question. Chris said the fix is to be direct and to ask about the problem itself. Chris also pointed out that Iwin closed right after that call and has closed six days in a row. JJ then took Lu through the She Creates webinar follow-up: call the P3 registrants to make sure they show up, and confirm each booked call when it appears in Slack.

Bottom line

What matters

The main lesson across both reviews is the same: find out why the prospect is really there before pitching, and ask about the problem in plain, direct terms. On Allan’s call the “I gotta think about it” came after the motivation was never fully dug into. On Iwin’s call a “why are you here” question landed badly, and a straight question about the problem would have been easier to answer.

JJ’s point on the second review was that the buyer should not feel like they are on a sales call. Iwin still closed straight after the call he was frustrated by, which is the habit to keep. Separately, Lu is set up in Slack and GoHighLevel and now knows which list to work before and after the webinar.
