The short version

What we covered

The call opened with logistics: a few reps had webinar lead calls booked and the team wanted a Webby lead call to study. JJ announced that Mr. Gab, who owns Mindtrepreneur, will come in on Thursday or Friday to run a mindset session on the subconscious side of selling, and said he would do it free to show the team what is possible. Harrison then volunteered a discovery call to review. His read was a decent discovery that did not create enough urgency, ending in a time objection: the prospect wanted to try it alone for three months and commit once he has more hours free. JJ watched it and said the time objection was the surface. The better move is to find the story the prospect is living in, name the gap between the old identity and the one he wants, and let him decide which one he is. Omar and Faris agreed the objection looked like a smokescreen, and Omar offered to follow up and close the lead.

Bottom line

What matters

When a prospect says he has no time, the objection is rarely about the calendar. JJ's point was that the call should surface the narrative the prospect is living into, then show him the gap between where he says he wants to go and the identity holding him back. Time will always be an issue, so the real question is whether he decides to be the person who makes the time.

For Harrison, the fix is to ask more consultative questions that build urgency around what is actually wrong with the content, and to get the decision from the prospect around the 20 minute mark so he handles his own objection at the end. JJ called this the Hail Mary that works when nothing else does.
