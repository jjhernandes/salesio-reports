The short version

What we covered

JJ opened the Saturday call with a reflection: even after a record week there are gaps, and the shift to a higher standard comes from a decision, not a moment. He covered the four ways to influence people, thoughts, feelings, behaviors and impulses, and said shifting behavior is what rewires you. Attendees then asked about a bad call taken with colleagues watching, how to reset between back-to-back calls, moving from B2C to B2B, and how to use heavy call feedback. The consistent answers were to treat a bad circumstance as an anomaly rather than a verdict, return to the fundamentals and buyer awareness, forget a bad call quickly like a goldfish, hold an expert frame with high-ego buyers by asking questions, and work on only one or two things at a time. JJ also said social practice is fine but warned against teaching manipulative tactics. Chris added why process matters even when you already understand sales.

Bottom line

What matters

A bad call in an odd circumstance is data about the circumstance, not about the rep. JJ's guidance: go back to the fundamentals, read where the buyer sits on the awareness scale, and do less to get more.

On calls back to back, treat each one as a clean slate. The next person has no idea how the last call went, so bringing that energy into it only costs you. Composure and frame control matter more than any pre-call routine.
