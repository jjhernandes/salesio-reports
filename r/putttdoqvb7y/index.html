The short version

What we covered

Brandon is closing for a security screens offer sold to contractors and contracting business owners. The package is a business in a box with a manufacturer partnership, training, and 110 prepaid leads for the 25k ticket. He has a full calendar, with 15 lives and nine offers so far, but most calls end with the prospect saying they need to learn more because they only found out about the offer recently. He told JJ that staying calm and asking what is missing worked, while pushing harder lost at least one prospect. JJ explained that he sorts buyers into high ego with low competence and high ego with high competence, and suggested treating contractors who know the product as people who only need the mechanism explained. He recommended supporting the research instead of fighting it, and using a free frame early so a prospect never feels pushed. They closed by covering the Content Academy group interview.

Bottom line

What matters

The main takeaway is to stop treating "I need to research" as an objection to beat. For a sharp buyer, telling them not to research attacks their identity and implies the offer is not real. JJ suggests guiding what to look for, being clear on next steps, and opening with a frame that says an offer might not come today.

The second takeaway is the buyer map. Know whether the person understands the product and how to run the business, and adjust tone accordingly. Brandon should become the expert on the one thing he is selling.
