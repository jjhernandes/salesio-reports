The short version

What we covered

JJ opened the Friday coaching call and Iwin pulled up a call from Nov 5 with a contractor prospect, selling content marketing for contractors in Southern California. Iwin's goal was to stop battling objections and start collaborating with them. Going through discovery, JJ pointed out that the call opened with a vague question, moved into the frame quickly, and skipped the rapport that might have opened up a guarded prospect. Daniel Garces asked about slowing the conversation down without looking fidgety, which led to a discussion of controlling cadence. JJ also pointed to a pitch that ran too long, and Iwin shared a quick example of cleaning up the language a prospect used when describing outreach versus inbound.

Bottom line

What matters

The objection Iwin ran into at the end of the call was shaped earlier. A vague opening, a rushed frame and a long pitch gave the prospect room to doubt, and the prospect later ghosted. Fixing those three things is likely to do more than any objection script.

The cadence of the call belongs to the seller. Slowing down is a choice, and it works when it is deliberate rather than nervous.
