The short version

What we covered

Hey Iwin, today you wanted my take on people-pleasing and why people do it. I said it works like a mechanism: people welcome you more when you please them, but it only wins them over for a moment, and once you stop it can feel like betrayal. How much power someone has over you comes down to how much value you give their opinion. You shared that you tend to bite off more than you can chew, promise a lot to land a client, then figure out how to deliver, and that you spread yourself across many things so you can be the guy who does it all. We talked about how you try to act like the people you look up to instead of just being you, and you noticed the gap between how you show up on a call with me and how you show up with prospects. That gap drains you after two or three calls. The plan is simple: keep doing what you're doing, and start writing out why calls do or do not go your way so we can review them together.

In my words

The real conversation

Iwin, the thing I want you to hold onto is this: “it's the amount of value that you give people that puts power over you.” People-pleasing feels safe, but it only buys you validation for a moment. The move is to ask yourself what you are doing that you do not actually need to keep doing, and to notice it in small places too, like whether you speak up when the table is not right.

On calls, I said it like this: “authentically selling is more or less like giving you permission to start to breathe like you and be who you are.” You do not need to become me or anyone else. Take what works from the people you respect, make it intentional, and let the rest go. And when a call goes sideways, “literally write it out when it does happen” so we can look at where it went wrong and why it threw you off.
