The short version

What we covered

JJ opened by asking the team to download an inbox app (Beeper) that puts Instagram, WhatsApp and iMessage in one place and supports automated follow-ups and pre-recorded voice messages. He said that setup helped him take two to four times more calls than other reps early in his career and close many through DMs. The team then lined up leads to follow up on: one lead to re-set with Malachi and a mental performance coach who did not show. The main block was a replay of a recent call with a software engineer interested in content. JJ paused it at key spots. Rapport was genuine, but it ran too long before the call turned tactical. He also coached going one level deeper when a prospect gives a surface answer, matching the prospect’s energy, and showing buyers who lead with ego what they have not considered yet.

Bottom line

What matters

The call review came down to timing and depth. Rapport is worth building, but there is a point where it starts doing more harm than good, and that is the moment to get tactical. When a prospect says something like wanting someone to review their videos, the useful move is to ask why, because the real need sits behind that answer.

For buyers who lead with confidence, the way in is to show them something they have not considered, early. People pay for a different perspective, so the rep should bring one.

What to fix

Turn rapport into tactical questions

Once the prospect opens up, steer into the real questions. Letting small talk run on makes it harder to bring the call back later.
