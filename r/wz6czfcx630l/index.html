What we covered

You wanted to find the leak, so we ran a full roleplay with me as a sophisticated, established contractor. Then we audited your real call with Gabbo, a contractor looking at ads for kitchens and new construction. Both showed the same pattern. You moved through current reality fast to get to pain, ran out of questions, and a few questions in it started to feel like an interrogation. Sophisticated prospects usually already know they need a change. That's why they booked the call. So the job isn't to push a pain that might not be there. The real conversation

You asked for the roleplay because you wanted to find the leak. In the roleplay and on the Gabbo call, the same thing showed up: you moved fast through current reality to get to the pain, ran out of questions, and it started to feel like an interrogation. You named it yourself: "I'm skipping through the discovery, because I'm trying to rush to the pain."

Like I said on the call: "it's just kind of staying surface level, and you're trying to push this pain, that maybe might not be there, especially if they're all making decent money."

And: "he was already aware that he needs to make a change. You don't need to convince him he needs to make the change."

And: "I'm not always looking to just go in there and find pain. All I'm trying to do is show them that I really understand exactly what's happening."

On the mindset piece: "I think you know what to do, but I think in a way, subconsciously, you're afraid to have that same thing happen again."

And: "I evaluate how I'm feeling before I think about what I'm saying. Because what you're feeling dictates what you're going to say anyway."

So ask why this, get depth before the next question, and get out of your head 🫡

What to fix

Get depth before the next question

Stay on an answer for two or three levels until you actually understand what's happening. Questions that build on each other feel logical. Rapid-fire questions feel like an interrogation.

Say it like this"So you want to do all three? Why do you want to do all three? Which one's the biggest revenue stream for you? Why not just focus on one?"
▶ 34:47

Ask why this, not what did you see

Asking what they saw puts them on the other side of the table. Asking why they want to be different lets them tell you why they're on the call.

Say it like this"Every contractor does the same things. Word of mouth, referrals. What makes you want to go against the grain and do something different?"
▶ 55:28

Restate, don't validate

Saying "makes sense" after every answer just validates what they're doing. Play back what you heard and keep going.

Say it like this"Okay, got it. Appreciate the context. So the majority of the revenue right now is from kitchens, just so I understand?"
▶ 36:48

Frame yourself like the authority they trust

Ease in with a calm, practical prospect. Think about what authority looks like to a contractor, like how their best clients behave, and match that.

Say it like thisAsk yourself: how would their clients behave? Show up like that.
▶ 29:22

Get out of your head

The fear of asking too many questions is what makes you rush. When a call feels uncomfortable, notice the feeling first, because it drives what you say.

Say it like thisWhy do I feel uncomfortable? Dissect it, understand it, move on.
