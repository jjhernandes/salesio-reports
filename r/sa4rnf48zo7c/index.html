The short version

What we covered

This was the Friday group coaching call. JJ opened with the standards for these calls: camera on, and a free chat where updates and recordings get shared so late-night call changes are not missed. He then reminded everyone that how much intensity you bring is a decision. The rest of the hour was a live review of a recorded sales call to a contractor. The student's goal was to stop battling objections and collaborate with them. JJ's feedback was that the problem started earlier: a broad opening question, rushing past a guarded buyer, and a long, technical pitch that de-sold the prospect. He closed by splitting buyers by ego and competence and explaining why, in B2B, you are usually selling to someone with high ego and low competence who needs an expert, not an argument.

Bottom line

What matters

The fix for objections is upstream. If the buyer is clear on what you do and why it works, there is far less to handle at the end. Ask specific questions, set the pace of the call yourself, and keep the pitch short enough that nobody tunes out.

When the buyer is a successful owner who does not know your field, do not fight their ego. Be the expert, explain it simply, and keep the fundamentals sharp: how you open, how you dress, how clearly you talk.

What to fix

Open with a specific question

A broad opener gets a vague answer back and makes the whole call feel ambiguous. Pick the topic, then ask for the overview of it.

Say it like thisGive me a 50,000 foot overview of what you're doing now with marketing.
▶ 25:22

Control the cadence, expect cold buyers

A guarded buyer gives one-word answers. Expect it, slow the opening down, and let the rapport build as the call goes on instead of rushing into the frame.

Say it like thisCool. Do you know how long she's going to be? Is it going to be forever, or are we talking five minutes?
▶ 31:27

Keep the pitch simple and under five minutes

A pitch that runs long and gets technical loses the buyer, and a buyer who is lost will say whatever ends the conversation. Dumb it down until it is easy.

▶ 39:29

Sort the buyer before you pitch

In B2B it is usually high ego and low competence: they know their trade, not yours. Do not out-ego them. Play the expert and explain it like they are five. Show up in a collared shirt, not a hoodie.
