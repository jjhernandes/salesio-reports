What we covered

The Core Problem: Perception & Authority

  • Sales success hinges on your perception of authority, not just your competence. ▶ 3:01
  • Your personal history and beliefs about authority (e.g., a stern principal) can create an intimidating "avatar" that subconsciously alters your sales behavior. ▶ 37:00
  • The Solution: Shift your behavior first to change the dynamic. Project competence unapologetically, regardless of the prospect. ▶ 5:45

Limiting Beliefs & Solutions

  • Brenda (B2B Transition): Believes she needs more business knowledge before selling B2B. ▶ 54:34
  • Solution: This is a limiting belief. B2B teams provide extensive training. The real challenge is managing conversations with high-level prospects, which requires projecting competence, not deep prior knowledge.
  • Iwin (Price Comparison Objection): Struggles with this common objection, feeling stuck. ▶ 59:09
  • Solution (Process): For B2B contractors, switch to a two-call close. Use the first call for discovery, then book a second for a demo and pricing. This leverages the "last option bias" (prospects often choose the last company they speak to) and reduces defensiveness.
  • Solution (Personal): A casual energy undermines authority with skeptical contractors. Adopt a more practical energy (professional attire, direct communication) to gain respect.
  • Matthew (Content Creation): Worries prospects will choose more established coaches. ▶ 68:51
  • Solution: Focus on providing value and using clear CTAs. A large market exists for coaches who offer personal attention, a key differentiator from famous gurus.
  • Alejandro's Insight: Balance your comparison bias by also considering less qualified coaches who are successfully selling. This builds conviction.
  • Sal (Anticipating Skepticism): Fears a prospect's skepticism will sabotage the call. ▶ 76:22
  • Solution: Reframe skepticism as a positive sign. It indicates a prospect is wrestling with their own fears. Your role is to help them overcome these internal blocks, not fight against them.
  • Torin (Product Knowledge): Worries about selling a niche offer without being an expert in that niche. ▶ 82:17
  • Solution: Your expertise is in sales and marketing, not the product itself. The sales process is universal. You will learn product knowledge over time, but it's not a prerequisite for success.

State Management: Detaching from Performance

  • Jackeline (Emotional Carryover): A bad call (caused by rushing) negatively impacted the next one. ▶ 87:34
  • Solution: This is a state management issue, not a flaw. The poor performance was a direct result of the frantic pre-call state.
  • Action: Attribute the outcome to the circumstance, not your value. Learn from the experience (e.g., allow more prep time) and move on.
