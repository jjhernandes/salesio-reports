The short version

What we covered

Iwin opened with a call from the day before. The prospect understood the offer and was ready to buy, but the price was $3,750 against about $2,500 from Spark Marketing, which he said included ad spend. Iwin pointed out that the competitor gives no guarantee and no coaching, but the prospect still said he needed to think about it after a call of over an hour. Chris had a custom $7,500 package that nearly closed on ClarityPay until the prospect's phone connection was too bad for the page to load. JJ tied both to the same lesson: contractors tend to be high ego and low competence on marketing, so the closer has to win the expert frame, stay more direct early, and ask questions that expose the gaps, such as how long they have relied on referrals and whether they track leads. Iwin said he would have more freedom to say no to someone like himself, which JJ confirmed is the point. Chris added that you can usually see the problem before they say it, so expose the gap, then tie belief back in. Iwin asked how to carry that authority, and JJ said his edge is that he knows more about marketing than the prospect does.

Bottom line

What matters

The main point for both closers: likability makes it easy for the prospect to say no. Winning the expert frame first creates the pressure to make a decision, and a direct start in the first minutes makes the rest of the call easier.

JJ and Chris will review the two recordings. Chris also shared a recording of JJ coaching him on respect over likability.
