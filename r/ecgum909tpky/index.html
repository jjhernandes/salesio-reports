The short version

What we covered

Chris opened by asking to role play the pitch for the new offer, because it is not landing the way he wants. He also walked through two recent pitches that stalled on logistics: one prospect needed his brother’s sign-off, another wanted to confirm the people going on camera first. Iwin said he handles the on-camera objection by telling prospects the team can arrange models or anyone willing to be on camera. Iwin then brought a call booked for tomorrow with a siding contractor whose revenue has been dropping and who blames the economy, and asked how to handle that on the call. JJ’s answer was to say what you would actually say, in your own words, and not soften it with extra questions. JJ also tied this to the guest speaker session earlier in the day: perception of competence matters more than competence, and trying to hide ego backfires. The call ended with Chris’s 3-month vs 1-month pitch role play and Iwin’s “give them something to chew on” reframe still open, to pick up after the group coaching call with Evan.

Bottom line

What matters

The main point JJ made is that directness lands when it sounds like you. Softening what needs to be said, or asking one more question to avoid saying it, is what lets the economy excuse stand.

On the siding contractor, the line is to name the excuse plainly and then give him room: he does not know what changed, and that is okay. JJ’s example for how to phrase it is in the fix below.
