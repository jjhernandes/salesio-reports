The short version

What we covered

Chris opened with a question: when a prospect refuses to open up in discovery, should you ever just walk away instead of pitching? He had followed this prospect for about a month, spoken to him six times, and got him on the call, but the prospect kept turning every question back on Chris and asked to be told what the offer does before sharing anything. JJ played back the start of the recording and read the pattern: a closed-off prospect who wants to stay in control. The fix is to give a very short pitch in the first minute so the prospect has context, then ask the discovery questions. Chris also laid out the two ways he sees people close (a clean discovery straight to price, or an emotional discovery with only logistical obstacles left) and admitted he has no third path yet. JJ will send a template for pitching first and then doing discovery. They also covered setting up GrokBot with an API key and location ID, and practising the new three-month investment pitch.

In my words

The real conversation

Chris, the instinct to stop pitching when the door feels shut is understandable, but with this kind of prospect the move is the opposite of walking away. As I told you: “You know, like a very slight pitch and like, okay, cool, like give him context. Like it's not the end of the world, you know? Like you would still win him over.”

The order matters. I said it plainly: “No, you pitch first, and then you would do the Discovery.” A guarded prospect who knows you are there to sell relaxes once he hears who you are and what you do, and then the questions feel like help instead of an interrogation. What to fix

Open with a slight pitch

When a prospect holds back in discovery, you ended up pitching with no information anyway. Give a short intro first so the prospect knows who you are and what you do, then earn the discovery.

Say it like this“Are you familiar with what we do here? Okay, well, just so you understand, here is who we are and what we do.” Then move into your questions.
