The short version

What we covered

Chris opened by sharing a map of the ways a call can end after the price is presented: a deal with logistical or money issues that needs a follow-up or deposit, partner objections, prospects comparing other programs, and last-second freezes. He asked JJ to confirm the list and name anything missing. JJ said an exit map is good to know, but it can add friction if you try to work out which route you are on mid-call, so the deeper dive will happen on their 1-on-1. JJ then focused on the ICP: business owners who are successful in their own field but newer to marketing and content. These buyers lose confidence in the sales rep late in the call when they do not see the rep’s expertise. The fix is to show authority early with prerequisite questions about ad spend, CAC and LTV, so the prospect sees what they have not considered and opens up in discovery. Iwin raised a concern that this could leave prospects less sure at the end, and JJ reframed it: doing something beats doing nothing. Iwin also shared a call he ran with two other reps watching. His discovery was solid, but he dropped the offer and the price back to back, so JJ suggested adding a clarity check between them.

Bottom line

What matters

The main decision is how Social Era reps open discovery. Instead of going straight into standard questions, reps ask a few prerequisite questions (ad spend, CAC, LTV) that let the prospect discover what they have not considered. That gives the rep authority early and makes the prospect more open for the rest of the call.

The second takeaway is the order at the end of the call: explain the offer, check how it lands and what would not work for the prospect, then give the price. Chris’s exit-route map and Iwin’s recent lost calls both move to their next 1-on-1 for a deeper review.
