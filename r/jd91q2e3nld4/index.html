The short version

What we covered

JJ opened with a one-on-one with Nana, who said her recent calls feel more natural since she started talking to prospects the way she would on the phone. JJ framed the next gap as perception: sales is a transference of belief, and prospects respond to perceived authority, not just competence. He pointed to the habit of making yourself smaller to make the other person feel bigger, and suggested a pattern interrupt or a direct, self-owned question when a prospect is guarded or short. The recording then moved into the Friday community call, which repeated the lesson on beliefs and authority, introduced the PACE method for adapting to a prospect, and took questions on moving from B2C to B2B, price comparison objections, content and lead generation, fear of skeptical prospects, selling an unfamiliar offer, and recovering from a rushed day. JJ closed by announcing a free WhatsApp group for recordings and a Saturday lesson on camera setup.

Bottom line

What matters

The through line of the call is that the obstacle on hard calls is rarely the prospect. It is the perception the rep brings in. Reps shrink around people who feel like authority figures, stay too soft or too bro-ish for the audience, and avoid saying the thing that needs to be said.

The practical moves are to adapt to the prospect (PACE), treat tension as the prospect working through their own fear, and change the process where the objection keeps repeating, for example by splitting a B2B sale into two calls.

What to fix

Making yourself smaller to make them feel bigger

Nana tends to over-soften with intimidating, serious prospects. JJ says that is a big reason deals slip, and that the fix is to stay yourself and ask the direct question.

Say it like this“I just feel like we haven't really connected. I think the whole thing is we're going to work together. There's an aspect of trust and vulnerability. And I just don't see that just yet.”
▶ 20:20

Go quiet on tension instead of naming it

When a prospect is short or guarded, use a pattern interrupt and put the question on yourself, not on them. Look at their hurt first instead of judging them.

▶ 13:12

Pre-handle price comparison in B2B

If most calls end on "I need to compare prices", change the process rather than fighting the objection in one call. Bring the topic up early and bring something new to the second call.

▶ 1:01:00

Fear of skeptical prospects

Skepticism is part of the job. Ask what they are afraid of (ROI, losing money, time, not being good enough) and sit on the same side of the table.
